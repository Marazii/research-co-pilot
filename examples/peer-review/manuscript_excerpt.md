## Abstract (excerpt)

This study examines how online communities shape trust in news media among young adults. Using a sample of 412 participants, we find that frequent community participation predicts increased trust in news sources (β = 0.34, p < .001). These results demonstrate that online communities causally increase media trust, with substantial implications for democratic discourse.

## Methods (excerpt)

Participants (N = 412, ages 18–34) were recruited from a university subject pool. After providing informed consent, they completed a 25-minute online survey measuring community participation frequency, demographic information, and trust in news media. We then regressed trust on community participation, controlling for age, gender, and education.

## Results (excerpt)

Community participation was significantly associated with trust in news media (β = 0.34, SE = 0.06, p < .001), with this association being significant for all subgroups examined. We additionally analyzed several moderator variables; the interaction with political ideology was non-significant (p = .12), suggesting the effect is uniform across the political spectrum.

## Discussion (excerpt)

These findings establish a strong causal link between online community engagement and media trust. The implications are clear: policymakers should encourage community-based news consumption to combat the trust crisis facing democratic societies. Future research should explore additional populations.
