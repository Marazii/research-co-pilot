# Editor's decision: Major revisions

Dear Authors,

After review by two reviewers, we have decided to invite revisions of your manuscript "How Online Communities Shape Trust" (J. Hypothetical Studies). Please respond point-by-point to all reviewer comments and submit the revised manuscript along with a tracked-changes version.

## Reviewer 1

**R1.1** The causal language throughout the manuscript is not supported by the cross-sectional design. The abstract claims "online communities causally increase media trust" but the methods establish only a correlational association. Please revise to use associational language consistently OR provide identification strategy for a causal claim.

**R1.2** The subgroup analysis is reported as significant "for all subgroups examined" without listing them, providing cell sizes, or addressing multiple-comparison correction. This is insufficient transparency. Please provide a full subgroup table with N per cell, effect sizes with 95% CIs, and indicate whether subgroup analyses were pre-specified.

**R1.3** The non-significant moderator finding (p = .12) is interpreted as showing the effect is "uniform across the political spectrum." This is absence-of-evidence vs evidence-of-absence error. Report the 95% CI on the interaction term and acknowledge power constraints.

**R1.4** The discussion's policy recommendations ("policymakers should encourage community-based news consumption") assume generalizability the data don't support. The college-aged sample doesn't represent the populations whose trust dynamics policy targets.

## Reviewer 2

**R2.1** I disagree with R1.1's framing — I think the causal language is fine if the authors clearly note the design's limitations. The bigger issue is that the literature review misses key prior work by Kraut & Resnick (2012) and others showing exactly this association in different contexts. The paper does not engage with this prior work.

**R2.2** The 25-minute online survey raises concerns about participant attention. Please report attention-check pass rates and provide sensitivity analysis excluding inattentive participants.

**R2.3** The trust measurement is single-source (one validated instrument). Convergent validity with a second instrument would strengthen the result.

## Editor's notes

In addition to the reviewers' points:
- **E.1** Please reduce manuscript length by ~15% to fit the journal's standard format.
- **E.2** Confirm pre-registration status — if not pre-registered, explicitly label all subgroup and moderator analyses as exploratory.
