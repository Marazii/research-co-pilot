# Genre lenses for peer review

Apply the relevant lens during Step 4 (reading) and Step 5 (writing). Genre is detected per submission and stated in the Header. For mixed-genre work, apply multiple lenses; if the genres pull in incompatible directions, name the tension as a finding rather than picking one silently.

## Empirical study

What it is: a paper presenting original empirical findings (RCT, observational study, qualitative interview study, mixed-methods design, ethnography, etc.).

Primary criteria:
- The research question is well-posed and falsifiable.
- The design is appropriate to the question (the right method for the question being asked).
- The sample is appropriate (size, recruitment, demographics, generalizability).
- Operationalization is defensible (how are constructs measured?).
- Analysis is appropriate to the design (right tests, multiple-comparison handling, effect sizes, confidence intervals, pre-registration where applicable).
- Findings actually answer the research question, and conclusions do not overreach.
- Limitations are honestly addressed.
- Reproducibility: data, code, and materials availability.

Common failure modes: question and method mismatch, underpowered design, overinterpretation, p-hacking, confirmation-biased framing, hidden multiple comparisons, ecological invalidity, ignoring confounds.

## Literature review or systematic review

What it is: a paper synthesizing existing research on a topic.

Primary criteria:
- Scope is well-defined (inclusion and exclusion criteria stated).
- Search strategy is documented and reproducible (databases, search terms, date range, language).
- The synthesis is more than a summary: identifies patterns, gaps, contradictions, evolution of the field.
- The author's own perspective is acknowledged where it shapes the synthesis.
- Engagement with prior reviews on the same topic.
- Quality assessment of the studies reviewed (not all studies weighted equally).

Common failure modes: undeclared scope (cherry-picking), pure summary without synthesis, missing major works, no acknowledgment of contested findings, no critical evaluation of the literature, no positioning of the review's own contribution.

## Meta-analysis

What it is: a quantitative synthesis of effect sizes across multiple primary studies.

Primary criteria:
- All criteria for systematic review, plus:
- Effect-size extraction methodology is documented and replicable.
- Heterogeneity is assessed (and explained when high).
- Publication bias is assessed (funnel plot, fail-safe N, etc.).
- Moderator analyses are pre-specified, not post-hoc data dredging.
- Subgroup analyses are appropriate.
- Statistical model (fixed-effect vs. random-effects) is justified.

Common failure modes: garbage-in-garbage-out (low-quality studies pooled with high-quality ones), heterogeneity ignored, publication bias unaddressed, post-hoc moderator fishing, pooling apples and oranges.

## Theoretical or argument paper

What it is: a paper advancing a conceptual claim, theoretical framework, or sustained argument, primarily on the basis of reasoning rather than original data.

Primary criteria:
- The thesis is specific, novel, and stated clearly.
- The argument is valid (the conclusion follows from the premises) and sound (the premises are defensible).
- Engagement with the strongest existing positions on the topic, not the weakest.
- The strongest counter-arguments are anticipated and addressed.
- Examples and illustrations are doing argumentative work, not just rhetorical work.
- Conceptual clarity: key terms are defined and used consistently.
- Implications are drawn out (what does the argument commit the author to elsewhere?).

Common failure modes: vague thesis, hidden premises, straw-manning opponents, argument-by-vivid-example, terminological drift, equivocation, not engaging with extant literature.

## Methods paper

What it is: a paper introducing or validating a method, technique, instrument, or measurement approach.

Primary criteria:
- The method is described in enough detail to be reproduced.
- Comparison to existing methods is fair and complete.
- Validation is appropriate to what the method claims to do.
- Limitations and failure modes are honestly discussed.
- A worked example or case study demonstrates the method in action.
- Code, materials, or instruments are made available.

Common failure modes: insufficient comparison to baselines, validation on the same data the method was developed on, hand-waving on edge cases, no discussion of when not to use the method.

## Case study

What it is: an in-depth examination of a single case (event, organization, individual, system) for theoretical or practical insight.

Primary criteria:
- The case is justified: why this case, why now?
- The case is described with enough specificity to evaluate the reading.
- Generalization claims are calibrated (case studies do not support universal claims).
- Reflexivity: the author's relationship to the case is acknowledged where relevant.
- Theoretical or practical contribution is articulated.

Common failure modes: overgeneralization from N=1, descriptive without analytic content, undisclosed researcher relationship to the case, neglecting alternative interpretations.

## Position paper, commentary, or opinion

What it is: a paper advocating for a position, often on contested or normative matters, sometimes in response to other work.

Primary criteria:
- The position is stated clearly and early.
- Arguments for the position are substantive, not merely assertive.
- Counter-positions are engaged seriously, not dismissed.
- Evidence (where applicable) is current and accurately represented.
- The author's stake or position is acknowledged.
- The piece adds something to the conversation (rather than restating the obvious).

Common failure modes: position is asserted rather than argued, counter-positions are strawmanned or ignored, the piece restates a well-known view without contribution.

## Dissertation chapter

What it is: a single chapter from a larger work, usually presented for review in the context of a thesis-in-progress.

Primary criteria: depend heavily on the chapter type (literature review, methods, results, discussion, theoretical, etc.). Apply the relevant other genre lens.

Genre-specific considerations:
- The chapter must integrate with the surrounding thesis (the reviewer should not assume the rest of the thesis exists, but should note where the chapter relies on context the chapter itself does not provide).
- Standalone-readability: can someone outside the committee understand the chapter on its own?
- The contribution-to-thesis question: what does this chapter add to the larger argument?

## Conference paper

What it is: a paper for a conference proceedings (typical length 8-12 pages, sometimes shorter for workshops).

Primary criteria: same as the relevant primary genre (empirical, theoretical, methods), but with awareness that:
- Length constraints force compression; criticisms about missing detail must be calibrated against the format.
- Conference papers are often early-stage work; the contribution claim should be sized accordingly.
- Reproducibility is harder to assess but no less important; supplementary materials matter.

## Workshop paper

What it is: a shorter, often more exploratory paper for a topical workshop.

Primary criteria: same as conference paper, but with even more tolerance for in-progress work, position statements, and provocations. Workshop papers are venues for ideas-in-development; demanding finished theses misreads the genre.

## Public-facing essay

What it is: a piece written for a general or semi-academic audience: substack post, magazine article, blog post, manifesto, op-ed.

Primary criteria:
- Accessibility: the argument is comprehensible to the intended audience without sacrificing rigor.
- The thesis is clear and stated early.
- Claims are sourced or qualified appropriately for the venue.
- The piece earns the reader's attention (this is a real criterion in this genre, not pandering).
- Engagement with the relevant scholarly literature where the piece makes scholarly claims.

Genre-specific considerations:
- The "Accept / Major / Minor / Reject" register is journal-shaped and may not fit. The verdict can be re-cast as "publishable as-is / publishable with changes / not yet ready / needs fundamental rework."
- Standards for citation are looser than for journal papers; this is acceptable, but distinctive arguments and unfamiliar empirical claims still need sources.
- Voice and authorial presence are valued in a way they are not in journal writing.

Common failure modes: smuggling academic argument into a public format without earning the rhetoric, or the inverse (overformalizing for a public audience), undefended technical claims, missing engagement with the obvious counter-positions, padding.

## Research proposal or grant proposal

What it is: a proposal for research not yet conducted, seeking approval or funding.

Primary criteria:
- The research question is significant and clearly stated.
- The methodology is feasible and appropriate.
- The proposer is positioned to do the work (preliminary data, expertise).
- Timeline and resources are realistic.
- Significance and contribution are articulated, not assumed.
- Engagement with existing work establishes the gap the proposal fills.
- Limitations and contingencies are addressed.

Common failure modes: overpromising, vague methodology, missing engagement with prior work, no clear deliverables, unrealistic timeline.

## White paper

What it is: a long-form report from an organization (industry, think tank, government) on a topic, often policy-relevant.

Primary criteria:
- The audience and purpose are clear.
- Claims are sourced (whitepapers often have less rigorous sourcing than journal papers but still need it).
- Conflicts of interest or institutional positioning are disclosed.
- The recommendations follow from the analysis.

Common failure modes: motivated reasoning toward predetermined conclusions, weak sourcing for strong claims, undisclosed institutional incentives.

## Replication study

What it is: a study attempting to reproduce the findings of prior work.

Primary criteria:
- Faithfulness to the original design (where possible) is documented.
- Deviations from the original are justified.
- Statistical comparison to the original is rigorous (effect size comparison, equivalence testing where appropriate).
- The interpretation is calibrated: failure to replicate is not the same as proof the original was wrong; success does not vindicate every claim of the original.
- Pre-registration is essentially mandatory for this genre.

Common failure modes: cherry-picked deviations from the original, underpowered for the comparison being made, overinterpretation of failed replication, no engagement with original authors' likely responses.
